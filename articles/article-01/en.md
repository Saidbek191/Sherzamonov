# Tajikistan Enters the Phase of a Family Power Transition

Rustam Emomali, the mayor of Dushanbe and son of the president of Tajikistan, has been nominated as a candidate for the city council. The voting will take place on March 1 — the same day as the elections to the lower house of parliament — and on March 27 the elections to the upper house will follow. This electoral cycle will conclude in November with the presidential elections. In my view, all these events are parts of one chain. The country is unfolding its own Tajik scenario of a power transition — carefully planned and meticulously executed.

## In the Spring of 2020, Tajikistan Once Again Holds Parliamentary Elections

I am convinced that the majority of seats will go to the People’s Democratic Party — the pro-presidential force that fully controls the political landscape. The only intrigue is whether the Social Democratic Party, which calls itself the “constructive opposition,” will manage to win at least one seat. In reality, however, the outcome for all parties is decided long before the elections themselves. Therefore, the main public interest is not in the parliament, but in the upcoming presidential elections — it is there, in the autumn, that the future of Tajik power will be determined.

Preparations for the transfer of power began long ago. In 2015, the parliament adopted a special law — “On the Founder of Peace and National Unity — Leader of the Nation.” It was created specifically for the current president, Emomali Rahmon. This law granted him the title of “Peshvoi Millat” (“Leader of the Nation”) and, more importantly, the constitutional right to run for president an unlimited number of times. Yet today, society increasingly discusses a different question — will Rahmon run again himself, or will he begin the Operation Successor.

Even if he decides to hand over power, his position will not weaken. The title of “Leader of the Nation” remains for life, and with it — the ability to control all key political decisions. In that case, the transition scenario will resemble Kazakhstan’s, though with national specificity: the focus will be placed exclusively on family members. Dushanbe is trying to avoid the mistakes of others — such as the Kyrgyz scenario, where the successor turned away from his patron, or the Kazakh one, where rivalry arose between the old and the new leaders.

## Rustam Emomali Has Been Prepared for This Role for Many Years

In November 2013, at the age of 26, he received the rank of major general. He then went on to hold a series of key positions — heading the Customs Service, the Anti-Corruption Agency, and the Committee on Investments and State Property. He also served as chairman of the Youth Union and tried himself as a deputy in the Dushanbe City Assembly. In January 2017, he was appointed mayor of the capital. In this post, he gained not only administrative experience but also the opportunity to establish contacts with international organizations, politicians, and even heads of state. For his diligence, his father awarded him the “Zarrintoj” (“Golden Crown”) order.

## However, to Fully Consolidate His Position Within the System, Rustam Emomali Must Take One More Step — Becoming Chairman of the Upper Chamber of Parliament

The path to this post runs through the elections to the Dushanbe City Assembly. As I mentioned earlier, all heads of regions, districts, and cities must be deputies of their corresponding administrative bodies. Therefore, the mayor of Dushanbe must also be a deputy of the city assembly. Heads of administrative entities cannot be members of the lower house of parliament, but they may be elected to the senate — and, without doubt, that is exactly what will happen.

Of the 33 members of the upper house, 25 are elected by secret ballot at assemblies of regional deputies, including Dushanbe, while eight are personally appointed by the president. Thus, there is little doubt that Rustam Emomali will replace Mahmadsaid Ubaydulloyev as chairman of the senate and become the second most powerful person in the state.

From that point on, everything will be a matter of procedure. Emomali Rahmon will likely run for another presidential term to finally clear the field of the old elite, and then step down ahead of schedule, transferring power to his son. Meanwhile, he will retain control over all processes and the right to intervene in any decision if he deems it necessary.

Not all analysts, however, share confidence in the heir’s smooth future. Some — such as Igor Pankratenko from the Center for Strategic Assessments and Forecasts — believe that Rustam Emomali is not yet ready for the presidency and “will need another ten years of preparation.”

Furthermore, the struggle between domestic clans is intensifying, and another figure is emerging on the horizon — Ozoda Emomali, the president’s daughter. Like her brother, she grew up within the system and advanced her career under her father’s guidance. She worked in the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, served as deputy minister, and later became head of the presidential administration. In 2016, she became a senator from Dushanbe and chair of the parliamentary committee on constitutional rights and freedoms. In 2017, she was granted the rank of state counselor of justice. Today, she is among the most influential women in Central Asia, and the international organization Women Political Leaders has appointed her as its ambassador to Tajikistan.

Thus, the Emomali family is not merely preparing for the transition of power — it has effectively already begun it. The only question that remains is who will take the throne first — the son or the daughter.
